Overall Aim
To improve the physical health, emotional well-being, and sense of belonging among families all ethnic and cultural backgrounds and without forgetting underserved families particularly those raising children with disabilities, by providing inclusive, joyful, and culturally relevant activities that strengthen family bonds and build resilient communities, as physical activity is medicine ,this project can have profound and positive impact on the NHS ,both in terms of health outcomes and cost savings ,reduces pressure on NHS sevices.
 
 
 Why this Aim is powerful and Touching:
This recognizes the invisible struggles, it speaks directly to the heart of social inequality, acknowledging that families from minority backgrounds and those with special needs are often excluded from mainstream services.
It commits to building joyful, safe, and inclusive spaces not just for children, but also for parents, carers, and even grandparents who may be carrying invisible emotional burdens.
It addresses the trauma of isolation, giving families a chance to be part of something bigger than themselves a community that understands, accepts, and uplifts.
The activities offered under this project are not just fun they are tools of healing, connection, and empowerment, especially through:
Shared movement (fitness sessions)
Creative joy (dance and music)
Safe expression (parent circles)
Cultural celebration (event days)
Neurodiversity inclusion (autism-friendly zones)
Ultimately, this aim envisions a space where:
“Children can laugh without being judged, parents can rest without feeling alone, and everyone can move, dance, and heal together where happiness comes first”
 

 

 

Specific Aims
 
1. To increase participation of underserved families, from all ethnic, cultural, and migrant backgrounds including those raising children with disabilities, in safe, joyful, and inclusive community activities.
 
2. To reduce emotional stress, loneliness, and social isolation among parents, carers, and grandparents by building a support network of peers and community allies.
 

 
3. To improve the emotional well-being, self-esteem, and confidence of both children and their caregivers through shared physical activities and creative expression.
 
4. To uplift and celebrate cultural identity and diversity, particularly among black, African, and migrant families, by incorporating culturally familiar music, dance, and celebration days.
 

 
5. To increase understanding and visibility of autism and other additional needs in the wider community through inclusive programming, awareness days, and neurodiverse-friendly spaces.
 
6. To create safe, non-judgmental environments where families feel they belong, feel proud of who they are, and are empowered to connect, learn, and grow together.
 

 
7. To foster stronger bonds between parents and children through shared play, movement, and joyful routines that model positive, active parenting.
 
8. To strengthen community cohesion and trust by developing a consistent, accessible family hub that becomes a go-to space for emotional support, physical activity, and inclusive fun.
 

 

 

Objectives (Project Activities)
 
These objectives represent the specific work that the project will carry out to achieve the broader change described in our aims.
 
1. To deliver inclusive, co-participation fitness and dance ,cardio ,palate sessions for families with children
Sessions will combine movement, laughter, and bonding.
Parents, children, and even grandparents will be encouraged to participate side-by-side in routines that are fun, non-judgmental, and adapted to include children with autism or sensory sensitivities.
E.g. ,Songs Used:
• “Fitness Barbie” by Sandra Pink Linda a motivational afro-pop workout tracks celebrating femininity, confidence, and movement. It promotes body positivity and joy, especially for mothers who feel unseen.
• “Hakuna Matata” Swahili for “No worries”, this classic encourages children to let go of anxiety and embrace happiness. Its rhythm and lyrics uplift everyone’s mood.
Impact: Encourages active parenting, boosts children’s self-esteem, and creates lasting positive associations with movement.
2. To run sensory play stations and calm zones for children with additional needs
 
Activities include water play, soft tactile blocks, textured fabrics, and interactive, non-verbal play.
Calm corners with dim lighting, cushions, and sensory tools will support children needing quiet space.
 
Impact: Enhances inclusion for children with autism and sensory processing needs, allowing them to fully participate without overwhelm.
3. To host music and dance sessions celebrating culture and togetherness
Includes live drumming, Afrobeat, traditional lullabies, and upbeat global rhythms.
Sessions promote cultural pride and intergenerational bonding through familiar rhythms.
 E.g. . Songs Used:
• “Jerusalem” (South African gospel-house hit) Celebrates hope, unity, and spiritual joy.
• Custom multicultural playlists blending Congolese, Caribbean, Afrobeat, and Western children’s songs.
 
 Impact: Helps families feel seen, respected, and emotionally uplifted through cultural expression.
 
4. To facilitate monthly themed celebration days that reflect inclusion and diversity
• Example days: African Pride Day, Autism Awareness Fun Day, Grandparents’ Day, and Sibling Celebration Day(birthdays)
• Activities will include dressing up, storytelling, dance-offs, inclusive games, and cultural food sharing, karaoke
 
Impact: Builds empathy, celebrates identity, and educates the community in a joyful, respectful way
5. To provide a safe space for parents and carers to share their experiences and receive emotional support
• “Circle of Support” groups led by trained well-being advisors.
• Topics include parenting stress, mental health, resilience, and navigating special needs support systems.
 
Impact: Reduces parental burnout and loneliness, while increasing confidence and coping skills.
6. To build a digital support and promotion network to keep families connected and engaged
• A WhatsApp group for real-time updates, reminders, and emotional encouragement.
• A Facebook group for parents to share wins, challenges, and peer support.
• An Instagram page for joyful reels, event recaps, and community visibility.
 
impact: Keeps families engaged even outside physical sessions and helps promote the project to wider audiences, including funders and potential partners.

Policy Statement
In the United Kingdom, safeguarding means protecting a person’s right to live in
safety, free from abuse and neglect. At Happiness comes first SCIO we understand this to mean protecting vulnerable people from any potential harm that could arise from
encountering our staff or activities.
This policy will address the following areas of safeguarding: -
 Vulnerable adult safeguarding
Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, Happiness comes first SCIO believes that every person that we encounter has the right to be protected from all forms of harm, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Happiness comes first SCIO is committed to ensuring that we as an organisation take all reasonable steps to do so.
This policy applies to anyone working on behalf of Happiness comes first SCIO. This
includes our Board of Trustees, paid staff and volunteers. This policy seeks to provide them with the principles that guide our organisational approach to safeguarding. It also outlines the actions which are required to be taken when dealing with any safeguarding concerns.
Responsibilities
The ultimate responsibility for this policy rests with the Happiness comes first SCIO Board of Trustees.
Happiness comes first SCIO commits to ensuring: 
 All concerns or suspicions of a safeguarding nature which arise in the
course of any work being carried out by Happiness comes first SCIO will be taken
seriously and responded to quickly and appropriately All trustees, staff and volunteers are clear about what their safeguarding responsibilities are .
All trustees, staff and volunteers know how to respond to any safeguarding
concerns in an appropriate manner
 All personal information is stored and recorded professionally and securely
with access of this information restricted to those that require it to carry out
their roles within the organisation
All vulnerable adults have the right to protection from all types of harm and
Abuse.
All vulnerable adults have the right to be treated with respect and dignity
 Staff of Happiness comes first SCIO will receive appropriate training with regards to
safeguarding at a level that corresponds with their role within Happiness comes first SCIO.
All reports of safeguarding concerns are followed up promptly

Effective complaints measures are in place within Happiness comes first SCIO
All trustees, staff and volunteers have duties in relation to safeguarding and must
know what action to take if they believe an individual to be at risk because of
issues which arise while Happiness comes first SCIO s work.
All trustees, staff and volunteers must: 
Report any concerns they may have with respect to safeguarding immediately
to the Board of Trustees.
Consider any potential safeguarding issues when planning any activities. Any
activities must be planned in a way that protects people from any risk of harm
that could potentially arise
Take all reasonable steps to ensure that they do not have unsupervised
access to vulnerable adults during their work with Happiness comes first SCIO
How to report a safeguarding concern
All staff and volunteers are asked to raise any concerns they may have with regards
to safeguarding through the following routes: 
Raising any concerns with the Board of Trustees as quickly as possible. If the
staff member or volunteer does not feel comfortable reporting to a specific
member of the Board of Trustees (For example, if that person is involved in the
concern) then they may report to another member.
If working in an external location (such as another organisation) and a
concern arises; they must report their concern directly to the external
organisations safeguarding lead officer as well as informing a member of
Happiness comes first SCIO’s Board of Trustees
 If the situation is urgent or immediately dangerous, all staff and volunteers are
encouraged to contact the police on 999
Confidentiality
The Board of Trustees will ensure that appropriate procedures are in place within the
organisation to maintain a record of any safeguarding concerns which are reported
along with any outcomes.
Happiness comes first SCIO will also ensure that confidentiality is always maintained when
dealing with any safeguarding concerns.
Any information which relates to the concern and any subsequent actions that have
been taken as a result) will be shared on a need-to-know basis only, and the
information will be always kept secure.
